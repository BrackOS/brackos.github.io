Working toward the x64 Canary

Our current focus is getting the x64 kernel ready for its first public build.

Why x64?

The x64 kernel is currently the primary focus of Brack OS development. It has replaced the original 16-bit kernel as the project's main target because it gives us a much more practical foundation for the operating system we want to build.

x64 is also widely supported by modern desktop and laptop hardware, making it a useful platform for getting Brack OS into the hands of more people.

Where we are now

Brack OS is still in early development. The kernel is not yet ready for a public Canary release, but development is active.

Current target: x64
Canary: Planned for around September 20, 2026
Stable 0.1: Targeted for around October 15, 2026

These dates are goals rather than promises. Kernel development can uncover unexpected problems, especially when hardware and drivers become involved, so the schedule may change.

What's left to do?

There is still plenty of work before a public build is ready. We need to continue improving the kernel, finding and fixing bugs, and making sure the system behaves reliably enough for other people to experiment with it.

Once a Canary build is available, testing and bug reports will become especially important. Getting Brack OS running on hardware we don't have access to ourselves will help us find problems much faster.

What's after x64?

x64 is only the beginning. ARM64, x86, and ARM32 are all planned targets, with RISC-V remaining a possible future direction.

Eventually, we'd also like to move beyond the kernel itself and develop more userspace software and, much further down the road, a graphical interface.

For now, though, we're focused on getting the x64 foundation right. One kernel, one bug, and one reboot at a time.
